Chapter 2 · Service failures

It's not the queue. It's the heat.

Queue time barely moves: a completed swap takes about 3.5 minutes in every season and at every hour. What fails is supply. "No charged battery" is about 60% of all failures, and it comes from old Gen1 chargers that slow down when the cabinet gets hot.

The 30 worst stations are all Gen1: 11 in Delhi NCR, 9 in Jaipur, 9 in Hyderabad and 1 in Bengaluru. They handle 31% of attempts and produce 42% of failures. 3W riders fail twice as often as 2W riders because each station holds only 0 to 6 3W slots.

Chapter 3 · Retention

Two bad swaps in week one, and they're gone.

Across 13,751 new riders, the clearest sign that someone won't return is a failed swap early on. The effect holds after controlling for city, partner, vehicle class, signup channel, battery health and how much they ride.

Chapter 4 · Batteries

One battery cohort ages three times faster.

Range follows battery health closely. A pack at 95% health covers about 67 km; at 65%, about 38 km. Three Kyron 2W lots bought for the 2024 expansion lose 9.6 health points per 100 swaps, against 3.0 for every other lot from every supplier.

Riders call it a vehicle problem: "scooter weak lag raha hai", "bike problem lag raha hai overall". Agents filed all 9,361 of these tickets as "other". Reclassified, battery range is the largest complaint category. And 36,109 swaps after 15 June 2025 still handed out Kyron packs already marked as retired.

ZipDrop's discount rose from 12% to 28% in November 2024, and it is exempt from the peak surcharge. Revenue per swap fell from ₹59.7 to ₹48.7, about ₹3.7M given up in eight months, with no change in its growth trend.

Chapter 6 · Expansion

New stations went where sites were easy.

Two waves added 60 modern stations, many at highway fuel pumps and residential sites. The three hot cities had 73% of summer failures but got half the new stations, and summer failure at their old Gen1 stations was unchanged a year later (13.1% to 12.8%).

Chapter 7 · The budget decision

Four proposals, checked against the data.

More stations

Not as proposed

Past waves grew volume but didn't fix the Gen1 hot spots. Upgrade Gen1 first, or add Gen3 capacity right next to those stations.

More batteries

Partly

Stock-outs come from slow charging, not too few packs. Spend on replacing the Kyron 2W cohort and adding 3W packs.

Network-wide peak pricing

Conditional

It shifts 2.5 to 4.5 points of demand and lifts revenue, but only where the payer sees the surcharge. Remove partner exemptions first.

Exclusive with the largest partner

No

ZipDrop is last of 12 partners on margin per swap, with 45-day payment terms and no surcharge. Renegotiate before any exclusivity.

Chapter 8 · What to do, in order

Fix the chargers first. Retention and margin follow.

  1. Cool or replace the 51 Gen1 cabinets before summer 2026

    Start with the 30 worst in Delhi NCR, Jaipur and Hyderabad. They caused about 45,000 extra failed attempts over two summers.

  2. Protect every new rider's first two weeks

    Route them to Gen2/Gen3 stations, reserve charged packs at peak, and step in after a second failed attempt.

  3. Replace the Kyron 2W cohort and enforce retirement

    Block retired packs from being issued, claim against the supplier, and test incoming lots.

  4. Renegotiate ZipDrop toward a 12-15% discount

    Add volume tiers, remove the surcharge exemption, and tie any discount to off-peak usage.

  5. Extend peak pricing to the hot cities, then target new stations at need

    Put new Gen3 capacity beside the Gen1 hot spots and 3W-heavy stations.

How we got here

Cleaned first

Removed 2 test stations, shifted 139,490 swaps logged 5h30m early by firmware v3.2.0, merged 21 city spellings into 6, and set odometer resets to missing.

Costed every swap

Margin = revenue minus grid energy, site rent and maintenance, and battery wear priced per health point, with pack life taken from actual end-of-life retirements (about 61% health).

Tested, not assumed

Difference-in-differences for pricing, logistic regression with a mediation check for churn, and keyword rules on English and Hinglish ticket text.

Observational data shows strong, consistent associations, not proven causes. Every number on this page comes from the accompanying Colab notebook.
